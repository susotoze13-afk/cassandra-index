 Germany orders over 150 Russian mission staff and relatives to leave 
 Friday, September 11, 2026 10:00:39 AM
 More than 150 staff members of Russian diplomatic missions and their family members have been ordered to leave Germany, following Berlin's decisions to close the Russian Consulate General in Bonn and end the activities of the Russian House in Berlin. The number of people affected by the German government's decision was reported by unnamed sources. 
 Berlin's move comes in response to an attempted sabotage at Leipzig/Halle Airport in early August. The German government has publicly blamed Russia for the incident. Moscow has denied any involvement. 
 A spokesperson at the Russian Embassy said the decision amounts to the "urgent expulsion from the country of over 150 officials and their family members" from Russian missions. German authorities have not disclosed an exact number of people required to leave. However, Germany's Foreign Ministry previously confirmed that all registered staff at the Russian Consulate General in Bonn must depart. 
 The Russian Consulate General in Bonn is set to cease operations on September 18. Germany has also terminated the agreement allowing the Russian House cultural center to operate in Berlin. Moscow continues to deny involvement in the incident. 
 Russia's Embassy described Berlin's decision as an "unprecedented" step aimed at undermining Russian-German relations. Officials in Moscow have argued that Germany has provided no evidence linking Russian state agencies to the attempted sabotage. "No evidence, no arguments, only the usual 'highly likely' rhetoric and an unprecedentedly inadequate response," the embassy said. 
 Russian diplomats have described a device found in Leipzig as a "broken drone of unclear origin, which caused no harm," and warned that Berlin's actions would restrict Russian citizens' access to consular services in Germany. 
 In response, Russia announced reciprocal measures. The German Consulate General in Saint Petersburg must end its operations no later than September 18, with staff required to leave Russia by that date. In addition, the branches of the Goethe-Institut will cease operations in Russia.
